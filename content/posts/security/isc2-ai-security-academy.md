ISC2 just launched the [AI Security Academy](https://www.isc2.org/professional-development/ai-security-academy), a set of certificates, courses and workshops built for security people who have to secure, govern or lead AI at work.

It's not generic "what is a large language model" training, and it's not vendor tool training. ISC2 is aiming squarely at the work security teams are already being handed: AI risk reviews, AI governance policies, AI in the SOC, and AI-written code in the pipeline.

**The short answer:** the Academy has three levels (Foundations, Practitioner, Leader). Each level has a few options, and finishing any one option unlocks that level's badge. You don't have to go in order. Most practitioners should start with the Practitioner certificate that matches their day job.

## What is the ISC2 AI Security Academy?

It's a menu, not a single course. ISC2 groups its AI learning into three levels and gives each level a badge:

| Level                        | Focus                                          | What unlocks the badge                   |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **AI Security Foundations**  | Shared baseline of AI security knowledge       | One certificate or one workshop (16 CPE) |
| **AI Security Practitioner** | Applied skills in a specific discipline        | One of four certificates (11 CPE each)   |
| **AI Security Leader**       | Executive decisions and organizational rollout | One of three workshops (16 CPE each)     |

Complete all three levels and you also hold the overall AI Security Academy badge.

Two details matter more than they look:

- **Any order.** ISC2 explicitly says you can start wherever your responsibilities are. A GRC lead doesn't need to sit through foundations first.
- **CPEs count beyond ISC2.** Eligible learning earns continuing professional education (CPE) credits whether you're an ISC2 member or you maintain a credential with another association.

## What's in each level?

### AI Security Foundations

Pick one:

- **Building AI Strategy Certificate (16 CPE).** Six courses: AI for Cybersecurity, AI Security: Managing Overconfidence, Aligning with Global AI Regulations, Foundations of AI, Planning for Secure by Design AI, and The Evolving Cybersecurity Workforce.
- **Securing AI: Cybersecurity Strategy Workshop (16 CPE).** Managing AI security risk and building a secure-by-design AI roadmap aligned to trusted risk frameworks.

(The earlier Securing AI: Cybersecurity Operations Workshop is retired. If you attended it, you already have the Foundations badge.)

### AI Security Practitioner

Pick one of four certificates, 11 CPE each:

- **Governance & Compliance.** Turning AI requirements into governance that works.
- **Risk Management.** Identifying, assessing and managing AI risk, and making defensible decisions.
- **Security Operations.** Securing AI-enabled environments and using AI responsibly in security work.
- **Software Security.** Securing AI-assisted development, from code to architecture.

This is the level with the most immediate payoff for most readers. Each certificate lines up with a real job family.

### AI Security Leader

Pick one workshop, 16 CPE each:

- **Organizational Management.** Turning AI governance into an executive strategy. Available now.
- **Governance, Risk and Compliance.** Governance that enables AI adoption without losing control of risk. Coming April 2027.
- **Software Security.** Building and deploying AI-enabled software without leaving security behind. Coming June 2027.

## Which path should you start with?

Match it to the work on your desk, not your job title.

- **You're in GRC or audit.** Start with **Practitioner: Governance & Compliance** or **Risk Management**. AI regulation and AI risk registers are landing on GRC teams first. If you lead the function, add the Leader GRC workshop when it opens in April 2027.
- **You're in the SOC or detection engineering.** Start with **Practitioner: Security Operations**. Pair it with hands-on practice, like the AI application safety lab in [CyberSHIELD Labs](/posts/os-weekly/cybershield-labs-hands-on-defensive-security/).
- **You're in AppSec or DevSecOps.** Start with **Practitioner: Software Security**. AI-assisted code is already in your repos whether or not anyone approved it.
- **You're a CISO, director or security manager.** Go straight to **Leader: Organizational Management**. You need a governance strategy you can take to the board, not another primer.
- **You're early in your career or switching in.** **Foundations: Building AI Strategy** gives you the vocabulary. But don't treat it as a door-opener on its own. (More on that in [Entry Level Cybersecurity Jobs in 2027](/posts/security/entry-level-cybersecurity-jobs-2027/).)

## Is it worth it?

It depends on what you need it to do.

**Where it helps:**

- **CPE time you'd spend anyway.** If you hold CISSP, CCSP or a credential from another body, you're already logging CPEs every year. Spending them on AI security is a better use than another generic webinar.
- **Role-specific, not hype.** Splitting the Practitioner level into GRC, risk, SecOps and software security reflects how AI work actually lands on security teams.
- **A visible signal.** Badges are an easy way to show a hiring manager or your own leadership that you've done structured AI security learning.

**Where to keep expectations in check:**

- **It's a certificate, not a certification.** There's no CISSP-style exam and experience requirement behind it. It shows you learned something. It doesn't prove you can do it. A project you can walk someone through still carries more weight in an interview.
- **Two of the three Leader workshops aren't out yet.** If the GRC or software security leadership track is what you want, it's a 2027 plan.
- **It's one option among several.** CompTIA SecAI+, ISACA AAISM and IAPP AIGP all target AI security and governance from different angles. Check which one your employer or target employers actually ask for.

**What to do about it:** if you have CPEs to earn this cycle, put them toward the Practitioner certificate that matches your job. Then pair it with one piece of real work, like an AI risk assessment, an AI acceptable-use policy, or a review of how your team uses AI coding tools. The certificate gets you the framework. The work gets you the story.

## Do this next

1. **Pick one Practitioner track** that matches what you do every week: governance, risk, security operations, or software security.
2. **Check your CPE cycle.** If you're short on credits this year, the Academy is a useful way to close the gap with relevant material.
3. **Ask your employer to pay.** Training budgets often cover CPE-eligible learning, and AI security is an easy business case right now.
4. **Turn what you learn into an artifact.** Write an internal AI risk register, a secure-by-design checklist for AI features, or a one-page AI governance proposal. That's what you'll talk about in your next review or interview.
5. **Bookmark the Leader dates.** GRC in April 2027, Software Security in June 2027.
